Research

My work spans four DOE projects — TIDES, Diaspora, Mochi, and RECUP — organized around three focus areas: Agentic AI for science, streaming and resilience, and provenance and reproducibility.

Focus 01

Agentic AI for science

The TIDES project focuses on data management for scientific Agentic workflows on leadership HPC systems. I lead the characterization of vector databases on HPC platforms and the design of provenance mechanisms to track lineage, quality, and reproducibility across Agentic workflows.

This work includes ControlA, control mechanisms for reliable agentic workflows, and research on federating AI co-scientists across laboratories with collaborators across DOE labs.

Focus 02

Streaming & resilience

My Ph.D. work, DEISA, coupled Dask analytics with MPI simulations for in situ and in transit processing. Within the Mochi project I contributed to the evaluation of persistent streaming with Mofka on DOE leadership facilities, using real scientific workflows as testbeds.

The Diaspora project targets resilient, end-to-end workflows spanning instruments, edge resources, and supercomputers. There I developed RESILIO, a resilient tomographic reconstruction pipeline using persistent streaming, and contributed reliability mechanisms for emerging Agentic workflow systems.

Focus 03

Provenance & reproducibility

The RECUP project focuses on making hybrid, distributed workflows reproducible through scalable metadata and provenance services. I led efforts on performance reproducibility for distributed task-based workflows on HPC platforms and developed performance and provenance capture methods for large-scale AI workloads.

I contribute to Flowcept, runtime provenance for AI and scientific workflows, and to PGT-I, memory-efficient distributed training of spatiotemporal graph neural networks.
